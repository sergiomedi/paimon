## The autonomy did not pay for itself

`investigator` cost **1.9× the tokens, 3.2× the latency and 3.2× the wall
clock** of the single-pass path, and scored below both simpler systems on every
category that is trustworthy.

Paired, task by task, over the same 30 tasks:

| | difference in pass rate | p | |
|---|---|---|---|
| investigator − incident-triage | **−28.0%** [−48.9%, −7.1%] | 0.012 | distinguishable from zero |
| investigator − answers | −21.3% [−48.4%, +5.7%] | 0.113 | not distinguishable from noise |
| incident-triage − answers | +6.7% [−10.9%, +24.3%] | 0.432 | not distinguishable from noise |

Only the first is significant. Against `answers` the interval crosses zero: on
30 tasks this dataset cannot separate them, and saying it can would be reading a
point estimate as a result.

**Nothing can be concluded from the multi-hop column.** It has **seven tasks**.
All three systems score 14–26% on it and every interval is wider than the
spread between them. Seven tasks is not a measurement of multi-hop ability; it
is the reason the held-out set exists.

## Why the investigator loses, and it is not what it looked like

The first reading was "it never takes a second hop": 1.093 tool calls per run,
and on all 35 multi-hop attempts **exactly one**. That is true, and it is not
the main cause. The cause is in the two categories where the other two systems
score 100%.

Every failed `investigator` attempt in one-hop and exact-identifier, classified
from its transcript — 24 failures out of 75 attempts:

| class | count |
|---|---|
| **withdrawn — no citation resolved** | **18** |
| wrong citation (right document) | 5 |
| wrong document | 1 |

Eighteen of twenty-four are the investigator's own `verify` node doing its job:
the model wrote an answer, none of its `[n]` markers resolved against the
evidence, and the answer was withdrawn as unsupported.

Across the whole run, on answerable tasks only:

| | citations / attempt | attempts citing nothing | withdrawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| `answers` | 1.49 | **0 / 125** | 0 / 125 |
| `incident-triage` | 1.54 | **0 / 125** | 0 / 125 |
| `investigator` | 1.48 | **37 / 125** | **37 / 125** |

Same model, same corpus, same day. The two systems that present sources as a
numbered block inside the user message produced a resolvable citation on **every
one of 250 attempts**. The system that presents passages as `tool` messages with
run-global numbering failed to on **30%** of them.

That is evidence against "the model cannot cite" and for "this harness makes
citing harder". It is not proof of a mechanism: the withdrawn text is
**not recorded anywhere**, because `verify` replaces the draft, so there is no
way to tell from these reports whether the model emitted no markers, markers out
of range, or markers in a form the resolver did not match. Recording the
withdrawn draft is the first thing to change, and it is an observability gap
rather than a cosmetic one — a run that says "could not tie the answer to any of
it" and keeps no record of what it tried cannot be investigated.

Withdrawals by category, answerable tasks:

| category | withdrawn | of |
|---|---|---|
| exact-identifier | 14 | 25 |
| injection | 10 | 15 |
| multi-hop | 9 | 35 |
| one-hop | 4 | 50 |

## Where the second hop actually went — a009

The flagship two-hop task. INC-3187 names RB-114 and deliberately refuses to
restate its steps; step three is in RB-114.

One search returned RB-114 — but the **`## Related`** chunk, which matched
because it names INC-3187. `read_document` would have returned step three. It
was not called. The answer:

> "they executed **Step three of RB-114**, which is the documented resolution
> procedure for such incidents"

— the postmortem's own pointer, restated, without ever reading what step three
says. The tool that would have closed the gap was offered on every turn.

## A grader that was wrong

`answers` scored **0%** on out-of-corpus, and I nearly wrote "single-pass RAG
never refuses". It refuses every time:

> "The provided sources do not contain specific information about the escalation
> path for a security incident involving customer data. **Therefore, I cannot
> provide an answer based on the given documentation.** [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8]"

That is a correct refusal. The grader read it as an answer because it carried
citations — the eight sources it had just called irrelevant.

| | |
|---|---|
| out-of-corpus attempts | 25 |
| prose that explicitly disclaims coverage | **25 (100%)** |
| graded as passing by the rule that shipped | **0 (0%)** |
| the same probe applied to answerable attempts | 0 of 125 |

The two populations are cleanly separable; the grader was not reading the
signal. It is now a judged classification, blind to the category and the
expected outcome, with a crude phrase probe kept as a cross-check and every
disagreement listed.

Three of the four stand-ins missed this because **all of them refuse in the
platform's canned words**. The grader was only ever tested against refusals it
was guaranteed to recognise. The fourth stand-in, added afterwards, refuses in
prose with citations — its text copied from the a024 transcript rather than
invented.

## A product finding, recorded and not fixed

The same a024 response, through the API's own serialisation:

```
grounded : True
citations: 8
answer   : The provided sources do not contain specific information about the
           escalation path... Therefore, I cannot provide an answer based on
           the given documentation.
```

`grounded` is computed as "did any marker resolve", and the README says *answers
carry citations or they are not returned*. Strictly the README's claim is about
the other direction and is not contradicted. What is misleading is that
`grounded: true` does **not** mean "this is an answer": a client filtering on it
would show this refusal as a grounded answer.

Deliberately not fixed during the measurement. A structured refusal — `grounded:
false`, citations dropped — is its own step, measured before and after.

## What this run cannot tell you

- **Multi-hop has seven tasks.** No conclusion about hopping is available from
  it, in either direction.
- **One model, one size.** Everything here is `qwen2.5:7b-instruct` on CPU.
  Whether a more capable model behaves differently in the same graph is the
  experiment, not an inference from this one.
- **The mechanism behind the 37 withdrawals is unproven**, because the withdrawn
  text is not recorded.
- **The out-of-corpus and injection columns are pending** the judged re-grade.
- **The judge is uncalibrated.** A sample was *labelled and adjudicated by
  Claude (Opus); accepted by the project owner as the reference, without
  independent human review*. That is agreement between two models, not the
  check ADR-0032 asks for, and no number here is described as human-labelled.
